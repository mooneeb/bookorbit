The Useful Observation

A quiet garden rewards a patient observer. Each visit reveals something that was easy to miss the day before.

A reader walks through a garden and records the shapes of leaves. Each observation supplies a little context for the next one.

A page is more than a location. Its margins, illustrations, type, and neighboring words help a reader remember what a mark meant.

The path begins beneath the trees, then winds past a low wall. By the time the gate appears, the reader has found several things worth remembering.

The notebook holds words and a little drawing of a leaf. Together they recall the shape, colour and setting of the discovery.

Some sentences are short. Others fill several lines and reach across the boundaries introduced by a column layout.

The wind changes, but the reader keeps the original observation. A new edition must not rewrite the meaning of an old note.

A careful comparison checks the entire visible excerpt, including its blank margin and the small registration marks in the illustration.

The path continues beside a wall. A few leaves collect in its shade while the rest of the garden waits in the afternoon light.

At the next gate, the reader stops to write. A single leaf has caught the light and deserves a closer look.

The most useful observation keeps its original context. A small mark can bring an old idea back into focus.

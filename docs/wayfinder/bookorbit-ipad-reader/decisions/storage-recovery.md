# Storage, backups and recovery

Complete proposal for [Set library scale, storage, backup, and export requirements](https://github.com/mooneeb/bookorbit/issues/12). Fourteen live choices are recorded below; the issue holds final shared-understanding confirmation and resolution status. Numeric engineering limits and acceptance targets are proposed for that complete review. This is a specification, not production code or a successful backup/restore result.

## Accepted choices

The [first round](https://github.com/mooneeb/bookorbit/issues/12#issuecomment-6371410842) establishes:

1. Download books only when explicitly chosen, with a clear Ready offline state.
2. Ask before removing chosen downloads. Disposable caches can be cleared automatically; notes and pending work remain protected.
3. Require a reading-note recovery backup exportable to Files, including editable ink, context and unsynced/conflicting work. A copy kept only on the iPad cannot recover a lost iPad.
4. Require app recovery backup only. A new readable PDF/text export is outside the first version; existing website exports remain existing features.
5. After server failure, lose at most 24 hours of already-synced work when restoring a backup. This requires completed automated backups outside primary server storage, not merely a daily scheduled job.
6. Keep reading-note Trash until explicitly emptied, without automatic expiry.

The [second round](https://github.com/mooneeb/bookorbit/issues/12#issuecomment-6371518636) adds:

7. App recovery includes notes/context and the download list, without complete downloaded books. Server backups separately cover complete library files.
8. App recovery files are unencrypted and have no password requirement.
9. Keep seven daily and four weekly server recovery points, deduplicating where supported.
10. Restore notes/context before the complete book library, targeting four hours once replacement infrastructure and backup access are ready.
11. Validate 20 downloaded books as the initial iPad working set. This is not a hard download-count cap; the server still targets tens of thousands of books.
12. A replacement iPad fetches historical assets for downloaded books and opened notes. Other historical assets remain server-recoverable; already-kept local notes and unsynced work remain protected.

The [final operational round](https://github.com/mooneeb/bookorbit/issues/12#issuecomment-6371711106) accepts a planned server-write pause of at most five minutes for consistent capture, aborting failed attempts and resuming writes. It also selects restic for server backups with a separate operator-held repository key/password. That repository protection does not encrypt the iPad recovery files.

The [note model](note-model.md), [sync contract](offline-sync.md), [native stack](native-stack.md) and [glossary](../../../../GLOSSARY.md) remain inputs. This decision does not change note identity, exact source binding, deletion/conflict semantics, the PDF/EPUB split, or the accepted free-signing lifetime.

## Evidence and interpretation

[Inspected storage evidence](../research/storage-baseline-facts.md) records committed byte/pixel sizes and current server limitations. Three physical editable drawings were 10,599 to 18,995 bytes; their full-canvas 2x ink PNGs were 45,372 to 68,848 bytes. Context PDFs were 14,162 to 53,880 bytes. The 1280 by 960 RGBA calculation is 4.688 MiB per tightly packed buffer, before renderer/encoder copies. These are small successful samples, not large-input budgets.

The bench's eight-million-pixel/16 MiB checks occur after expensive allocation, while the exporter could permit a 256 MiB single raw buffer. Production limits must be checked before rendering/decoding where possible. Existing import acceptance up to 4 GiB does not establish native reader support, parser extraction, archive expansion or recovery capability. Current annotation exports omit native ink and stop at 5,000 rows. None of those exports is the required recovery backup.

[Recovery mechanism facts](../research/recovery-mechanisms-facts.md) distinguish a consistent SQLite/database copy, immutable-file protection, repository integrity checks and actual application recovery. No previous fixture gate is reopened, and no production performance or restore success is implied.

## Downloads, accounting and space pressure

Keep separate accounting for original book files; expanded EPUB members; native note originals; previews/context; pending/conflicting/recovered assets; database/indexes; transfer staging; and disposable render/cache files. Count actual unique on-disk bytes and report shared references without charging the same immutable local blob twice. EPUB estimates include original ZIP plus expanded members, manifests and temporary transfer space. A count of 20 books does not estimate their bytes.

Downloading one book is a cancellable job with resource/byte progress and status lookup. Verify original source and member hashes, required book resources and active-note/context availability before announcing the combined book-and-notes Ready offline state. If book reading is ready before notes finish, state Book ready; notes downloading. Keep historical source/context and unresolved note versions distinct from current-book resources. Trash assets not already local are fetched when that trashed note is opened; they do not force hydration of the entire account. A book change during preparation invalidates the package cut rather than mixing editions.

Use a proposed 10 GiB adjustable download budget and 512 MiB disposable disk-cache budget initially. The download budget is a user-controlled planning threshold: show total estimated bytes and ask to raise it or remove chosen downloads, never silently remove books. There is no book-count ceiling. Protect a one-GiB free-space reserve from new downloads, optional hydration, render caching and backup staging; do not treat that reserve as proof an in-progress save can succeed. Queue one book download at a time, alongside the two asset transfers already allowed by the sync contract.

Remove download clears the book's original/resource package only after confirmation; retained note/context and unsynced work remain. Note asset copies do not expire automatically after syncing or after closing the book. An explicit Remove local note copies action is available only for verified server-backed, unmodified assets not required by a chosen offline book or protected local draft/conflict/recovery/operation. Explain their continued server availability and obtain confirmation. It is distinct from Move note to Trash and is never a response to a background cache eviction.

At low space, clear only disposable caches first and stop new allocations/jobs that are not needed to preserve work. Keep existing downloads and notes accessible. If a durable save fails, retain the working draft while possible, report Save failed with actionable space/retry/recovery options, and never display Saved on iPad or Synced. Before uninstall/reset/container replacement, provide the required recovery backup and verify its completion. A failed export is not permission to erase the store.

## Initial negotiated resource limits

These are initial supported-envelope choices, subject to final confirmation and implementation stress checks. They are neither measured maxima nor guarantees that every malformed document inside a byte ceiling renders safely. Enforce server and client bounds, checked arithmetic, actual streamed bytes and cancellation. Reject unsupported input with a specific reason; never silently truncate text, crop ink, split a user's note or lower its fidelity to satisfy a limit.

| Boundary                    | Proposed initial limit and behavior                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| --------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Native EPUB original        | 512 MiB; management may still handle files the native reader cannot open.                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| Native PDF original         | 4 GiB, file-backed; at most 20,000 pages. Do not read the whole file into Data.                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| EPUB package                | 20,000 entries, 2 GiB total expanded members; validate paths/entry types and count actual output before Ready offline.                                                                                                                                                                               |
| Complete member consumption | 4 MiB per XHTML/CSS document; 32 MiB per other buffered member. Stream verification before passing complete text/Blob values to Foliate.                                                                                                                                                             |
| EPUB images/section work    | At most 16 million decoded image pixels and 8,192 pixels per side; at most 100,000 DOM nodes per section. Ten-second load/parse deadline with process/reader recovery, rather than blocking native controls indefinitely.                                                                            |
| Editable supported drawing  | 32 MiB encoded, 20,000 strokes and one million path points. Native archive decoding is still a whole-drawing boundary requiring stress validation.                                                                                                                                                   |
| Ink preview                 | Default 2x of the recorded logical crop, at most eight million pixels and 4,096 pixels per side, 16 MiB encoded PNG. Preflight geometry and pixel arithmetic before creating buffers. One render/encoder job at a time.                                                                              |
| Saved context               | Self-contained PDF/excerpt, at most 64 MiB per context asset. Preserve full required page/canvas margins and exact context; otherwise leave preservation incomplete.                                                                                                                                 |
| Text and metadata           | Typed body 256 KiB UTF-8, exact selected quote 64 KiB, individual manifest 256 KiB. Oversized selections/edits receive explicit refusal, without changing the existing note.                                                                                                                         |
| Metadata transport          | At most 50 operations per batch and 100 change/snapshot records per page as already accepted, plus 4 MiB decoded JSON per response/request. Fill fewer items when bytes bind; return continuation, never incomplete success. Assets are not inline base64.                                           |
| Asset transport/staging     | One-MiB application chunks, two concurrent note asset uploads. At most 512 MiB unreferenced upload staging per account and two active publications; capacity failure is retriable and never publishes half a note.                                                                                   |
| Management work             | Catalog/annotation pages at most 100 initially; explicit selections at most 250 IDs per request, job work batches at most 100, two worker tasks per user and four globally. Large all-matching selections become server-owned snapshot jobs with paged results, never a full client/server ID array. |

The resource loader needs response-byte, decoded-image, DOM/parser and deadline controls in addition to lazy section loading. Limits that cannot be enforced before platform decoding require isolated/process-recovery behavior and measured acceptance. Neither file size nor a timer alone guarantees WebKit/PDFKit memory safety. The unsupported state preserves native controls, source identity and existing notes.

Oversized newly created ink is retained locally as an incomplete candidate and can be reduced using erase/Undo, retried after a supported upgrade, or carried in recovery. Keep the previous complete server revision current. A recovery archive may transport an unsupported/oversized original opaquely within its separate import limits; it must not decode it or claim it editable. Do not replace it with PNG. New drawing tools/versions and preview profiles require capability agreement, without mutating old authoritative bytes.

For the initial supported corpus, target a combined native/WebKit resident working set of at most 512 MiB, including render/encoder work, measured with separate processes accounted for. Record GPU allocations separately where instrumentation exposes them. Bound active PDF pages to the visible page plus one neighbor each way; render page content in bounded tiles where required. This is an acceptance target, not a proven OS hard limit. Oversize/fault fixtures must fail recoverably without a retry loop after process termination.

## Retention, leases and replay safety

Retain current note parts, saved context, unresolved conflicting versions, Trash, Recovered changes, pending operations and every referenced asset while their owning state remains. Restoring a conflict or note is not a reason to collect its alternatives before the explicit resolution is durably accepted. Keep no promise of unlimited ordinary edit history or persistent Undo across relaunch.

Proposed collection and history settings:

| State                                            | Initial policy                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Unreferenced replaced/abandoned ready note asset | Seven-day grace after its last reference/lease is gone, then bounded collection. Active/recoverable/pending references always win.                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| Upload staging                                   | Receiving lease 24 hours from latest verified progress, bounded by the staging quota; expired staging is retryable. Commit/backup leases prevent collection through publication.                                                                                                                                                                        |
| Full operation receipts                          | Preserve until contiguous durable client acknowledgment; retain for seven additional days, then compact to replay floors/digest barriers. Never expire an unacknowledged gap merely by age.                                                                                                                                                             |
| Idle device incarnation                          | After 180 days without successful reconciliation, retire it before reclaiming its unacknowledged full receipts; returning devices must reconcile with a new incarnation before rebasing any safe work.                                                                                                                                                  |
| Receipt budget                                   | 50,000 unacknowledged full outcomes per device, 256 MiB per account. Pause new affected operations with a reconciliation/storage instruction at pressure; do not silently erase receipts or pending work.                                                                                                                                               |
| Change feed                                      | Thirty days, bounded further at 100,000 events or 128 MiB per account. Compaction invalidates old checkpoints explicitly; mirror rebuild preserves uncertain/local changes.                                                                                                                                                                             |
| Bootstrap/backup leases                          | Bootstrap 30 minutes, one per device/two per account, bounded renewal to two hours. Local recovery capture/asset lease two hours per attempt. One server capture attempt at a time; five-minute write gate, then a staged-cut lease renewed on verified progress for at most 24 hours. Expiration aborts safely and never creates a valid backup point. |
| Permanent deletion                               | Remove content from normal/recoverable server views immediately. Retain minimal deleted-ID/key-generation and retired-incarnation/replay barriers, without note bodies, while needed to reject delayed operations. No time-only tombstone expiry.                                                                                                       |

Backup points are independent historical copies under their selected retention. Deleting live content does not rewrite old backup points. Storage views distinguish current, Trash/conflict, pending/staging, historical backup and collectible bytes. Quotas never implement automatic Trash expiry. Admin-configured note/staging capacity pauses new publication rather than deleting retained content; the initial validation reserves at least 20 GiB for note/context assets and separate staging space, without asserting the running server has that capacity.

Garbage collection is owner-scoped, indexed and batched at 100 candidates. Recheck references, publication/backup leases and generation at deletion. No age test alone can make a file safe to collect. Keep replay barriers indexed and compact; their growth is accounted separately from content/history. Any later barrier compaction requires a generation/reconciliation procedure that preserves the confirmed rejection of stale creates, updates and deletions.

## Unencrypted iPad reading-note recovery

Use a versioned BookOrbit recovery container built from system AppleArchive encode/file streams, without AEA encryption or a password. The recovery mechanism research's encrypted option is not chosen. Version 1 uses an uncompressed archive stream and ordinary archive files/directories only, no symlinks, hard links, clones, devices or executable payloads. Its manifest records account/server/source/schema provenance, cut time, record/asset counts, lengths and SHA-256 digests. Store opaque assets once by digest, with bounded streamed records/references. It is an app restoration format, without a promise that a browser, Android app or another reader can edit PencilKit bytes.

Include the note store's logical current state: typed text/highlights, exact selectors and PDF geometry, native originals with version provenance, matching previews, self-contained context, source/book associations, pending immutable operations/dependencies/digests, receipt/checkpoint/incarnation state, unresolved conflicts, Trash, Recovered changes and competing reading positions. Include cached identifying book metadata and the chosen-download list. Exclude complete original book files and expanded book packages, disposable caches, Keychain, passwords, access/refresh tokens, cookies and credential-bearing transport configuration. Source quotations and retained annotated pages remain necessary note context, rather than complete prior editions.

Capture finalized drawing changes first. Establish a durable backup asset lease under the same serialization as publication/collection, then use GRDB 7.11.1's online backup to an isolated temporary database, on a background task with positive page stepping/progress. The selected DatabaseQueue still blocks its own writes during that copy: page stepping is not permission to interleave those writes. Bound the database-copy pause to five seconds; cancel and offer retry if it cannot fit, preserving the draft and live database. Never hold the writer for the much longer asset/archive export.

Derive the asset set from the frozen database, keep its references leased while writes resume, and stream verified files into the recovery archive. The snapshot must be credential-free; project it into a clean recovery database when needed, omitting unused/free-page contents and non-recovery transient rows. Preserve immutable pending payloads exactly. A raw database copy is not redaction. Close database/archive handles, verify every required entry and reopen the complete output for validation before reporting completion. Release the lease on success/cancellation, with restart cleanup after a crash.

Proposed import/export bounds are 64 GiB per recovery file, one million ordinary entries, two GiB of recovery database data, 256-byte normalized path keys, a 256 KiB prologue/summary, one-MiB streaming buffers and one worker. Use disk-backed paged asset inventories, not one in-memory manifest of all files. Count actual decoded bytes; refuse duplicate/colliding paths, traversal, unknown entry types, unbounded compression and inconsistent hashes/counts. Individual assets beyond active-reader limits remain opaque with explicit status. Check provider/staging free space before starting; export an incomplete attempt under a temporary name and publish only after validation. Larger sets require an explicit supported-format/limit change; do not label a partial file a full backup.

Use a staged, completed file for the Files handoff. Report success only after the destination provider finishes the coordinated write and the available read-back/count/hash verification succeeds. Display the backup's capture time and scope; a file kept inside the same app container is not the external recovery copy. Allow the user to choose an accessible Files destination without requiring a paid cloud service. A copy in On My iPad remains device-local.

Restore into a separate private staging store; never extract directly over the live database. Validate schema, account/server provenance, entry containment, hashes, references and supported encodings before switching. Reauthenticate to the matching account/server, reconcile restored incarnation/sequence/receipts and current lifecycle/part heads, and retain uncertain work for review. Importing a rolled-back local backup never silently replays old operations, restores permanently deleted notes, or resets the entire server's recovery generation. Local-only recovered work stays readable/editable where supported while offline; pending sync waits for the required verification. In-place import preserves the current store until the user reviews the recovered contents and the new store is complete.

When the server is unreachable, a validated file can open in an isolated offline recovery workspace. Its claimed account/server fields are provenance to verify later, not authorization to merge with an existing account or upload. Network activity remains disabled for that workspace until matching sign-in and reconciliation succeed. Preserve the current account's store and recovered file throughout.

Backups are manual through an explicit Save recovery backup action and required before a procedure that could erase local-only data. Show the last successful external backup time alongside local/pending counts. Automatic note sync remains separate; a normal weekly in-place renewal preserves data without deleting/reinstalling. If signing expires before an unsynced backup exists, recover app access through the accepted stable-identity route before attempting export. No software backup can restore unsynced work lost with a device before any external copy was saved.

## Coordinated server backup

Use restic 0.19.1 as the initial reviewed tool version and PostgreSQL logical dumps appropriate to the deployed major/pgvector extension. Pin operational tools when packaging the runbook and review upgrades. The repository lives on another device/storage failure domain, not the primary database/app/books disk. Local/SFTP/self-hosted REST destinations are sufficient; no paid subscription is required. Keep the repository recovery key/password and access details recoverable separately from the failed server. The iPad does not need that repository password.

Back up the database and necessary global roles, immutable note assets, full library book files, durable app-data assets, identity/capability/schema manifests, deployment configuration and applicable decryption keys. Exclude regenerable delivery/render caches and unrelated machine secrets. A directory copy of live PGDATA is not the selected database backup. Current users' existing whole-book notes/journals and library state are included through the whole-server database backup even though the iPad reading-note file does not add their offline owner.

Whole-server backup/runbook access belongs to the server operator; it is not a normal user's reading-note export. Any application-triggered backup, restore, key/configuration or deletion operation needs explicit backend administration permission and ownership/scope checks. Client-hidden controls alone are insufficient. A user's app recovery file contains only that verified account/server scope.

The proposed portable capture procedure is:

1. Pre-stage book/app files by verified immutable content and prepare the bounded reference inventory while the service is online. Do not count that preliminary staging as a valid recovery point.
2. Acquire a single coordinated write gate, drain active mutations/jobs, and prevent scanner, importer, metadata/file writers and external mutable-book writers from changing the captured state. Reading remains available. Native notes saved meanwhile stay local/pending; the server must not acknowledge blocked writes as accepted.
3. Within five minutes, capture the consistent database cut, finalize the matching file/version inventory and copy only final changed/missing bytes into stable backup staging or a verified coordinated filesystem snapshot. Check all required note and library references. Snapshot capability is not assumed to exist on the current host. If mutators/external writers cannot be coordinated, or the deadline/reference checks fail, abort the cut, release the gate and report failure.
4. Release live writes once the consistent staged cut is complete. Stream that immutable cut to restic outside the gate; protect the cut/leased source bytes until repository completion. Compression/upload must not prolong the write pause. Interrupted/incomplete captures are not valid backup points.
5. Treat restic incomplete-source exit status as failure, verify snapshot inventory and application reference/hash manifests, and record cut time, completion time, status, sizes and restore-tool/schema versions. Only a fully completed off-primary point counts toward recovery objectives.

This preparation/gate contract needs real scale validation. It does not assert that copying an entire large library or dumping an arbitrarily large database can finish in five minutes. A deployment unable to meet the bound must provide tested coordinated snapshots/pre-staging or report the requirement unmet; it cannot silently extend the pause or relax consistency. Local snapshotting alone is not the off-primary backup.

Preflight staging capacity: a portable copy can require another full library-sized allocation. A verified copy-on-write snapshot can avoid that copy, but is deployment-specific. A hard link to a mutable live file is not an immutable backup cut. Keep staged cuts private/read-only and preserve them until repository completion or explicit abort; one failed large attempt must not grow unbounded parallel staging.

Schedule an attempt every 12 hours to leave headroom for capture/transfer and a failed attempt, retaining at least seven daily and four weekly points. Retention applies only after a new valid point exists; do not prune the last known-good recovery point because a scheduled attempt ran. Warn when the newest completed point's captured state is 18 hours old and report the 24-hour target as missed at 24 hours. Include failure/backlog status in operator diagnostics; Synced still means durable server acceptance, not inclusion in a backup taken later.

Run repository structure checks after completed captures, a full restic data check at least monthly, and a full application recovery drill at least quarterly and after relevant schema/format/runbook changes. Data-subset checks between full checks may reduce load but do not replace complete coverage. Retention/pruning is serialized with backup maintenance and uses dry-run/reviewed policy before enabling automatic deletion. A repository integrity check is not evidence that notes are editable after restore.

## Recovery order and acceptance targets

Restore to a clean target and validate database/schema, asset manifests, ownership and hashes before exposing recovered state. Restore note/context data first, then rotate the sync recovery generation before normal client reconciliation. Preserve the server identity and account/source associations while making the changed history explicit. Previous client receipts and local work are compared, not blindly replayed.

Book metadata may exist while full book-file restoration continues. Show Source restoring and keep saved context available; do not treat absent staged files as deliberate book removal. Disable destructive missing-file scans and source-changing management for unrestored libraries. Re-enable those paths only after the matching file inventory is verified. A replacement iPad reauthenticates and hydrates assets for its chosen books/opened notes; existing local recovered candidates remain protected.

The four-hour notes/context target starts once replacement infrastructure, supported app/database tooling and repository access/key are available. It includes restoring the note-bearing database/app assets, integrity checks, generation rotation and a real iPad/browser note/editability check. It excludes acquiring hardware/accounts, the free-signing bootstrap for an unavailable installation, and completion of the entire book library. Record those prerequisites separately rather than hiding their delay.

Fault and boundary cases have exact correctness outcomes rather than averaged success. Use generated or appropriately private representative fixtures without publishing personal notes or books.

The final handoff must carry the resource/capability refusal states, backup/recovery runbook, dataset/measurement conditions and actual pass/fail evidence. Book-merge behavior remains the separate [Choose how book merges preserve notes and personal book state](https://github.com/mooneeb/bookorbit/issues/21) prerequisite. This decision does not close that question or the complete-journey review.
